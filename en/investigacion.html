Research and development

R&D

We study the human holobiont: the host genome and its microbiome, as a system that responds to the bio-exposome (diet, exercise, rest and environment). We develop tools to apply that knowledge in precision health.

Research lines

1 Genome-microbiome interaction and lifestyle

Relationship between host genetic variation, the composition and function of the gut microbiome, and phenotypes of nutrition, physical activity, sleep and circadian rhythms. It is the common framework of the other lines.

2 Microbiome-based precision nutrition

Design of dietary recommendations from the microbiome profile using a patented method (Phymofood, EP 4 224 486 B1). Applications studied: type 2 diabetes prevention in older adults and microbial production of conjugated linoleic acid.

3 Microbiome and colorectal cancer

Characterisation of tumour-associated microbiota and integration of genetic, epigenetic, microbial and lifestyle data to study cause-effect relationships and guide prevention strategies.

4 Genetics, microbiome and physical performance

Genetic variants associated with power and endurance, microbiome dynamics in athletes during the season or at altitude, and individual response and recovery profiles.

5 Long-read sequencing and bioinformatics

Full-length 16S rRNA sequencing with Oxford Nanopore (MinION), species-level taxonomic classification, quality control and statistical analysis of omics data, including functional data analysis.

7 patents · selection

Phymofood: designing diets from the microbiome

A computer-based precision nutrition system that translates each person's gut microbiome profile into a proposal of foods and food combinations.

European patent EP 4 224 486 B1, granted on 5 August 2026. Holders: EHU and University of Cantabria. Inventors: Odriozola Martínez A, Álvarez-Herms J, Merino Valdeolmillos R, Tirnauca C, González Benito A.

  1. Microbiome profileRelative abundance of the person's bacteria.
  2. Target bacteriaBacteria absent or at low abundance that should be favoured.
  3. Knowledge baseFoods, their prebiotic components and the substrates each bacterium uses.
  4. Food rankingOrdered by the potential effect of each food on the target bacteria.
  5. Food combinationSelection of complementary foods using hierarchical clustering.

From ranking to combination

The ranking shows which foods, individually, most favour the target bacteria. But two very similar foods feed the same bacteria, so adding both contributes little.

The system therefore computes, for each food, a profile of its potential effect on each target bacterium and groups foods by the similarity of those profiles using hierarchical clustering (UPGMA). Foods that join early in the tree are redundant; foods on distant branches complement each other.

The plan combines the top-ranked foods with foods from different groups so that, together, they favour as many target bacteria as possible. The professional then adapts the proposal to the person's preferences, health and goals.

The method has been applied in the group's studies, such as the type 2 diabetes prevention intervention (Nutrients, 2025) and the analysis of foods for bacteria producing conjugated linoleic acid (Nutrition Reviews, 2025), and in the microbiome service reports. Its efficacy for disease prevention requires controlled trials.

Selected publications

Summary of each study and its practical implications. Figures are our own simplified diagrams, not the original figures; numbers and methods are in the linked article.

Why Are Long-Read Sequencing Methods Revolutionizing Microbiome Analysis?

González A, Fullaondo A, Odriozola A. Microorganisms. 2025;13(8):1861.

Review of how long-read technologies overcome limitations of short-read sequencing (taxonomic resolution, variant detection, assembly contiguity) in microbiome studies. It provides a practical tool for choosing a platform and discusses amplicon versus metagenomic approaches and sequencing depth. It concludes that no single method yet combines all desirable features.

doi:10.3390/microorganisms13081861

In Search of Healthy Ageing: A Microbiome-Based Precision Nutrition Approach for Type 2 Diabetes Prevention

González A, Fullaondo A, Odriozola A. Nutrients. 2025;17(11):1877.

Review of the gut microbiota associated with type 2 diabetes prevention and a pilot study in 13 healthy adults over 62. After three months of a diet guided by a bioinformatic food-recommendation system (Phymofood), the relative abundance of beneficial taxa such as Faecalibacterium and Butyrivibrio increased, and overweight modified the response. It is a small study without a control group and does not demonstrate disease prevention.

doi:10.3390/nu17111877

Conjugated linoleic acid metabolite impact in colorectal cancer: a potential microbiome-based precision nutrition approach

González A, Fullaondo A, Rodríguez J, Tirnauca C, Odriozola I, Odriozola A. Nutrition Reviews. 2025;83(2):e602-e614.

Review of the evidence on conjugated linoleic acid (CLA) and the gut bacteria that produce it in relation to colorectal cancer. As an approach for further research, it proposes enhancing endogenous CLA production through microbiome-based precision nutrition. It does not propose a treatment.

doi:10.1093/nutrit/nuae046

New Insights into Mucosa-Associated Microbiota in Paired Tumor and Non-Tumor Adjacent Mucosal Tissues in Colorectal Cancer Patients

González A, Fullaondo A, Navarro D, Rodríguez J, Tirnauca C, Odriozola A. Cancers. 2024;16(23):4008.

Analysis of 130 paired tumour and adjacent non-tumour tissues from colorectal cancer patients (University of Navarra Biobank), sequencing the V3-V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene on MinION with NCBI taxonomic classification. Genera such as Fusobacterium, Leptotrichia and Streptococcus were more abundant in tumour tissue, and Bacteroides and Corynebacterium less so. These associations improve our understanding of colorectal-cancer microbiota; on their own they do not prove causality.

doi:10.3390/cancers16234008

The Gut Microbiota Characterization of a World-Class Mountain Trail Runner During a Complete Competition Season: A Case Report

Álvarez-Herms J, Burtscher M, González-Benito A, Corbi F, Odriozola-Martínez A. Journal of Athletic Training. 2025.

Case report with six samples across five months of competition. Microbiota diversity increased during the season, and short and long races were associated with different changes. It suggests that microbiota analysis before and after competition could inform recovery; as it concerns a single person, the results cannot be generalised.

doi:10.4085/1062-6050-0143.24

What if gastrointestinal complications in endurance athletes were gut injuries in response to a high consumption of ultra-processed foods?

Álvarez-Herms J, González-Benito A, Corbi F, Odriozola A. European Journal of Applied Physiology. 2024;124(2):383-402.

Narrative review proposing that part of the gastrointestinal complaints in endurance sports may relate to additives and simple sugars in supplements and ultra-processed products, acting through the microbiota and the gut barrier. It recommends tailoring nutritional strategy to each athlete's gut function.

doi:10.1007/s00421-023-05331-z

Possible relationship between the gut leaky syndrome and musculoskeletal injuries: the important role of gut microbiota as indirect modulator

Álvarez-Herms J, González A, Corbi F, Odriozola I, Odriozola A. AIMS Public Health. 2023;10(3):710-738.

Review of the evidence linking the microbiota, increased intestinal permeability and low-grade systemic inflammation with musculoskeletal injury recovery. Diet, prebiotics and probiotics are discussed as possible modulators. The proposed link is indirect and requires experimental confirmation.

doi:10.3934/publichealth.2023049

The GALNTL6 Gene rs558129 Polymorphism Is Associated With Power Performance

Díaz Ramírez J, Álvarez-Herms J, Castañeda-Babarro A, Larruskain J, Ramírez de la Piscina X, … Ahmetov II, Odriozola A. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research. 2020;34(11):3031-3036.

In 85 physically active men, T-allele carriers achieved 5-7 % higher power in the Wingate test than CC carriers, and the T allele was more frequent among power athletes in a Russian sample. It is the effect of a single variant on a polygenic trait.

doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000003814
