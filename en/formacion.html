Microcredentials in genomics and microbiome

Official EHU training, with ECTS credits recognised across the European Higher Education Area, for professionals who request, receive or interpret genetic and microbiome analyses and need to apply them with scientific judgement. Courses are taught in Spanish.

Pathway

The microcredentials form a progressive pathway: the first establishes the foundations and their interpretation; the second focuses on professional application.

Who it is for

Professionals in nutrition, medicine, biology, sport science, nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy, biochemistry and biotechnology who want to understand and interpret genomic and microbiome analyses. No prior knowledge of advanced genetics or bioinformatics is required.

Learning outcomes

  • Understand the molecular and functional basis of genetic and microbiome variability and its implications for health, nutrition, physical performance and circadian rhythms.
  • Interpret genetic and microbiome reports and integrate them into professional practice through real and simulated cases.
  • Critically appraise the quality of studies in genetics and microbiome research.
  • Use specialised databases and bioinformatic resources.
  • Design personalised interventions in nutrition, physical activity, rest and preventive health, respecting ethical and scientific principles.

Programme

  1. Getting started

    What genomics and the microbiome are, the course project, practice with scientific articles and Ensembl, and ethics and legal framework of genetic testing.

  2. Evidence and molecular basis

    Why genomics and the microbiome matter, ClinVar practice, quality criteria and search strategies, from DNA to variant.

  3. Techniques and bioinformatics

    GWAS Catalog practice, genetic and microbiome techniques, genetic bioinformatics (with polygenic score practice) and microbiome bioinformatics.

  4. Evolution and the digestive tract

    Genetic and microbiome evolution, the digestive system and intestinal permeability.

  5. Nutrition and sport

    Microbiome and nutrition, nutrigenetics, genetics and sport, microbiome and sport.

  6. Rhythms and sleep

    Circadian genetics, sleep genetics, circadian microbiome and sleep. Project presentations.

The 1st edition ran from 20 April to 5 June 2026 (EHU code 014-26).

MC2 · Pre-registration open

Applied interpretation of genetic and microbiome analyses: from report to intervention in health, nutrition and sport

The second microcredential of the pathway focuses on professional practice: how to read a genetic or microbiome report, what level of evidence supports each result, and how to turn it into a concrete, measurable intervention in nutrition, supplementation, training and rest.

Planned contents

  1. Tools to read a report

    Heritability, polygenic scores, Mendelian randomisation and levels of evidence applied to reading results.

  2. Host, drugs and supplements

    Host genome-microbiota interaction. Pharmacogenomics and supplementation, and referral criteria.

  3. Applied sport

    Microbiome and performance in practice. Gene doping: current status and detection.

  4. Mental health and the gut-brain axis

    Genetics of emotional response and the relationship between the microbiome and mood.

  5. Workshops: from report to intervention

    Integrated cases with genetic reports (health, nutrition, clinical biochemistry and sport) and microbiome reports, with intervention design and follow-up. Final project.
